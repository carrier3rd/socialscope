# OverRustle archive, second pass: predictions (written Nov 3, 2026, 9:40 PM, before the pass was built or run)

Three parts: an event timeline from daily word bursts, hostility aimed at streamers broken down by where the senders
come from (the hate-raid question), and how people write long "effort" messages in each chat compared with Reddit.

Definitions, fixed now:
- Effort post: a message of 25+ words, not counting emotes. Reddit comments use the same cutoff.
- Directed at the streamer: names the streamer (per-channel alias list in the script), or speaks to "you" without
  opening with a chatter's name or an @.
- Hostile: directed at the streamer and containing an insult, a slur, or a hostile phrase (lexicons in the script).
- Home community of an account: the chat where it sent most of its archived messages (20+ messages total, and that
  chat at least half of them); otherwise "mixed".
- Hate-raid candidate day: in one chat on one day, 15+ hostile streamer-directed messages from 5+ accounts of a
  single outside home community, at least 5x that community's usual daily level in that chat, with most senders new
  to the chat (first seen within 7 days).

## Event timeline
E1. Election night 2016 (Nov 8-9) is among DGG's 10 busiest days by message count.
E2. At least 3 of DGG's 20 most bursty days have a debate opponent's name as the top bursting word.
E3. Ban-related bursts ("banned", "bannedstiny", "unban") lead at least two days in Destiny's chats.

## Hostility by origin
H1. In every chat with enough data, most hostile streamer-directed messages come from the chat's own regulars:
    the home community's share is over 60%.
H2. Hate-raid candidate days are rare: under 1 per 100 logged days in every chat.
H3. In Hasan's chat (2018-2020), Destiny-home accounts send hostile streamer-directed messages at no higher a rate
    per message than Hasan-home accounts.
H4. Destiny gets more hostility from his own chat than other streamers get from theirs: his chats' hostile-directed
    rate per 10k messages is the highest of the large chats.

## Effort posts, and chat vs Reddit
L1. Effort posts are under 2% of messages in every Twitch chat and 2-5% in DGG.
L2. DGG effort posts use more argument markers (because, therefore, however, evidence, source...) per 100 words
    than any Twitch chat's effort posts.
L3. Chat effort posts use more second-person words per 100 words than Reddit comments of the same length.
L4. Reddit comments use more hedges (I think, probably, maybe, seems...) per 100 words than chat effort posts.
L5. Profanity per 100 words is higher in chat than on Reddit, for both all messages and effort posts.
L6. 2026 DGG effort posts (live log) look more like 2019 DGG effort posts than like 2026 r/Destiny comments on
    argument markers, hedges and second person.
